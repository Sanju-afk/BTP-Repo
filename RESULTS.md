# Results

The simulator is calibrated to AAPL, 2012-06-21 (LOBSTER, 10 levels). The parent order is a sale of
X = 6,000 shares within T = 10 minutes, in N = 20 decisions of 30 s each. That is about 18% of the
market-order volume expected over 10 minutes in L and M (55 shares/s), and about 8% in H (124 shares/s). Fees are $0.0030 taker and $0.0020 maker rebate, and latency is 50 ms.
The risk penalty is λ' = 0.5 bps per interval at full inventory.

Training: 5 seeds per method, 250k environment steps each (PPO collects whole 2,048-step rollouts, so its
runs stop at 251,904 steps, 0.8% more), with hyperparameters chosen under an equal
tuning budget. Evaluation: 1,000 episodes per condition (500 for the sensitivity runs in section 8), with the
same episode seeds for every policy.

Shortfall (IS) is in basis points of X·S0, net of fees; lower is better. The *objective* is IS plus the risk
penalty, which is what both RL agents were trained to minimise. Intervals are 95% stratified-bootstrap
intervals, resampling seeds and then episodes; comparisons with TWAP are paired by episode.
Source files are `out/report/report.txt` and `out/report/extra.txt`.

## 0. Reproducibility check (14 Oct 2026)

The whole evaluation was repeated in a clean copy, from the saved models and calibration files only, with
NumPy 2.5, PyTorch 2.14, numba 0.68 and pandas 3.0 (`./reproduce.sh`, about 35 minutes on 2 cores).

* All 572 policy-and-condition mean shortfalls (396 main, 80 AC world, 96 sensitivity) equal the shipped logs.
* `report.txt` and `extra.txt` regenerated byte-identical; every CSV in `out/report/` is numerically identical.
* `validate_sim.py` with 3,000 seeds reproduces `validation.json` exactly.
* `experiments/demo.py` reproduces the demonstration episode (test episode 7, models of training seed 0).
* Not repeated: calibration and training from scratch (about 4 hours); the results rest on the saved models.

## 3. DDQN vs PPO (main research question)

These are mixed-trained agents. Each cell is PPO minus DDQN with its 95% interval; a negative value means PPO is better.

| Condition | Mean IS | Objective | P(a PPO run beats a DDQN run on IS) | CVaR95, DDQN / PPO |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A: High | +0.39 [-0.00, +0.75] | +0.06 [-0.30, +0.42] | 0.12 | 22.6 / 21.0 |
| B: Medium | +0.35 [+0.03, +0.67] | **-0.63 [-1.01, -0.26]** | 0.08 | 23.7 / 19.3 |
| C: Low | +0.37 [+0.02, +0.71] | **-0.61 [-1.04, -0.19]** | 0.04 | 25.0 / 20.3 |
| D1: H→M→L | +0.15 [-0.23, +0.53] | -0.26 [-0.62, +0.10] | 0.24 | 23.6 / 21.8 |
| D2: H→L shock | +0.28 [-0.16, +0.70] | -0.08 [-0.49, +0.31] | 0.20 | 24.4 / 22.6 |
| D3: Markov | +0.53 [+0.21, +0.86] | -0.25 [-0.60, +0.12] | 0.00 | 22.6 / 19.5 |

The two algorithms make different trade-offs. DDQN reaches a lower *mean* shortfall: the difference is significant in L, M
and Markov, and borderline in H. PPO reaches lower *tail risk* and a better value of the objective both were trained on:
the difference is significant in L and M and not distinguishable elsewhere. The mechanism is in the actions. DDQN
posts limit orders for about 80% of its child orders, against about 60% for PPO. DDQN therefore completes less before
the deadline (completion 0.88 to 0.95, against 0.96 to 0.98) and carries more inventory into the forced sweep at T,
which fattens its tail.

Learning curves are in `fig_learning.png`. On validation episodes under the equal tuning budget, the best PPO
configuration scored 3.89 and the best DDQN configuration 4.93.

## 5. Robustness and adaptation (deck gaps 4 and 7)

| Method | Degradation ratio, IS(L) / IS(H) | Shock surge, IS(shock) - IS(H) | Inventory left at the shock | Adaptation index, shock / H→M→L |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TWAP | 1.74 | +0.86 | 50% | 0 / 0 |
| VWAP | 1.54 | +0.85 | 48% | 0 / 0 |
| POV | 1.61 | +0.27 | 30% | -0.37 / -0.65 |
| AC | 1.58 | +0.02 | 4% | 0 / 0 |
| DDQN (mixed) | 1.70 | +0.25 | 18% | **-0.14 ± 0.02 / -0.29 ± 0.01** |
| PPO (mixed) | 1.60 | +0.14 | 9% | -0.05 ± 0.01 / -0.07 ± 0.02 |

The adaptation index is the change in trading pace after the switch, in TWAP slices per interval, minus the same
policy's change at that step when no switch happens. Values are means ± standard error across the 5 seeds.

Both RL agents protect themselves against deteriorating liquidity mainly by trading early, not by reacting to it. When
the shock arrives, PPO has 9% of the order left and DDQN 18%, against 50% for TWAP. DDQN re-paces after a switch more
than PPO does, and every DDQN seed re-paces more than every PPO seed.

## 6. Value of observing the latent regime

The oracle agents are given the true regime label. Oracle minus observable-only objective, for the 12 combinations
of 2 algorithms and 6 conditions, ranges from -0.39 to +0.26 bps. Every 95% interval includes zero. The observable
microstructure features (spread, depth, order-flow imbalance, volume) carry the useful liquidity information, which
supports treating liquidity as latent (Macrì and Lillo, 2024).

| Condition | Ranking in the AC world (best first) | Ranking in the LOB (best first) | Kendall τ-b (p) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L | AC, AC-adaptive, PPO, DDQN, {POV = VWAP = TWAP} | PPO, AC, DDQN, AC-adaptive, VWAP, TWAP, POV | 0.62 (0.06) |
| M | AC, AC-adaptive, PPO, DDQN, {POV = VWAP = TWAP} | PPO, DDQN, AC-adaptive, AC, VWAP, TWAP, POV | 0.41 (0.21) |
| H | AC, AC-adaptive, PPO, DDQN, {POV = VWAP = TWAP} | DDQN, PPO, AC-adaptive, AC, POV, VWAP, TWAP | 0.31 (0.35) |
| H→M→L | AC, AC-adaptive, PPO, DDQN, {TWAP = VWAP}, POV | PPO, AC-adaptive, AC, DDQN, POV, TWAP, VWAP | 0.49 (0.13) |
| shock | {AC-adaptive = AC}, PPO, DDQN, {TWAP = VWAP}, POV | PPO, DDQN, AC-adaptive, AC, POV, VWAP, TWAP | 0.39 (0.22) |

Braces mark exact ties. In the AC world volume is deterministic and the regime is known, so some baselines produce
identical schedules there. τ-b accounts for ties. With 7 methods none of the τ values is significant at 5%, so the
evidence that the rankings change rests on two other results. First, AC is optimal in the idealised world, as the
theory predicts, but in the calibrated book an RL agent ranks first in every condition. Second, the transfer
penalty below is significant.
Agents trained in the AC world and deployed in the book lose 0.68 to 1.66 bps of objective against agents trained in
the book, and every interval excludes zero.

The conclusions do not change. RL beats TWAP in every variant. PPO has the better objective in M in every variant,
with every interval excluding zero. Under the shock the two are indistinguishable on the objective. DDQN's lower mean
shortfall holds as a point estimate everywhere and is significant in M in three of the four variants; under the shock
it is not significant in any variant. "Resilience ×2" is the variant `out/calib/regimes_fastres.json`, with the
fundamental-price tilt κ doubled. It reverts impact faster, closer to the data's 2 to 5 minute reversion.

## Answers to the deck's research questions

1. **Does dynamic liquidity change performance and trajectories?** Yes. Costs rise from H to L for every method:
   the degradation ratio is 1.5 to 2.0 across methods and 2.3 for DDQN trained on H. Trajectories change too: the
   agents trade earlier in transition episodes.
2. **Which achieves lower shortfall per regime?** DDQN has the lower mean shortfall in every regime as a point
   estimate; the difference is significant in L, M and Markov switching and borderline in H. PPO has the lower
   risk-adjusted cost in L and M and is tied in H. On mean shortfall both beat TWAP, VWAP, POV and the calibrated AC
   in every condition. On the risk-adjusted objective PPO is below AC in every condition, while DDQN ties AC in L
   (6.46 vs 6.45) and is above it in H→M→L (4.47 vs 4.34).
3. **Which is more robust to deterioration within the episode?** PPO has a smaller shock surge (+0.14 vs +0.25 bps)
   and lower CVaR. DDQN changes its pace more after a switch. Neither is significantly better on the objective under the shock.
4. **Do rankings change between the idealised and the data-calibrated simulator?** Yes. AC is first in the AC world
   (tied with AC-adaptive under the shock) but second to fourth in the book, where an RL agent is first in every
   condition. Rank correlation is moderate (τ-b = 0.31 to 0.62, not significant with 7 methods). Policies trained in
   the AC world lose 0.7 to 1.7 bps of objective when deployed in the book, and every interval excludes zero.
