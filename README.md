# Liquidity-aware RL for optimal execution (BTP implementation)

The project follows the B.Tech project deck *Liquidity-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Optimal Trade
Execution under Dynamic and Non-Linear Market Impact*. It compares DDQN and PPO with classical strategies
in a limit-order-book simulator calibrated from historical data. The comparison covers high, medium and low
liquidity, and liquidity that deteriorates during the execution. Where earlier work has a documented flaw,
the implementation fixes it; `docs/research_gaps.md` lists each gap, its source and the fix.

| Metrics: IS, total cost, realised impact, completion, risk, degradation, cross-regime (12) | `experiments/evaluate.py`, `experiments/report.py` |

## Data

`data/lobster/` holds the free LOBSTER sample AAPL 2012-06-21: NASDAQ, 10 levels, about 400k messages. It is
mirrored on GitHub because lobsterdata.com cannot be reached from the build machine. Any other LOBSTER
message/orderbook pairs can be passed to `liqexec.calibrate`, and regimes are then pooled across days.

## Run

```bash
pip install numpy scipy pandas numba torch gymnasium matplotlib
python3 tests/test_sim.py        # simulator and environment invariants (8 s)
python3 experiments/demo.py      # live demo: one shock episode, TWAP vs AC vs DDQN vs PPO (6 s)
./reproduce.sh                   # every reported number from the saved models, no retraining (about 35 min)
./run_all.sh                     # whole pipeline from raw data, including training (about 5 h on 2 cores)
python3 -m liqexec.fit_check out/calib/regimes.json   # simulator realism table (1 min)
```

The LOBSTER CSV files are distributed separately (`liq-exec-data.zip`); put them in `data/lobster/`. They are
needed only for calibration and validation, not for `demo.py` or `reproduce.sh`.

## Simulator in one paragraph

Each side of the book keeps its 10 best occupied price levels. Background order flow follows a
queue-reactive model (Huang, Lehalle and Rosenbaum, 2015). Limit orders, cancellations and market orders
arrive at rates that depend on the queue size, with a separate set of rates for each liquidity regime.
Queues are indexed by occupied level, not by tick distance, because AAPL in 2012 had a median spread of
15 ticks. Market orders cluster as a Hawkes process, using the branching ratio estimated from the data.
Their signs have long memory, generated by background metaorders with Pareto-distributed lengths (Lillo,
Mike and Farmer, 2005). After each trade, quotes revise on both sides (Hasbrouck, 1991). Liquidity
providers anchor to a latent fundamental price, which absorbs part of every trade's impact. The agent's
orders go through the same book: market orders walk the bids, and limit orders queue (join the queue, or
improve the price by one tick) and fill partially. There are latency and maker/taker fees. All parameters
come from maximum-likelihood estimates on the data or from simulated-moment fits; `out/calib/msm.log` and
`out/validation/validate.log` give the fit.

## Results

`RESULTS.md` has the tables. Figures are in `out/report/`, the research-gap mapping is in `docs/research_gaps.md`, and the
calibration and validation logs are in `out/calib/` and `out/validation/`. Trained models (5 seeds × every configuration) are in
`runs/`. The per-episode evaluation CSVs (`out/eval*/`, about 120 MB) are not included; `run_all.sh` step 8 regenerates them.
